## The journey in brief

[[distance-card id=shirdi]]

This is the most travelled route to Shingnapur. Many visitors to Shirdi's Sai Baba temple add Shingnapur as a half-day or full-day excursion. For the numbers and route comparison, see [Shirdi to Shani Shingnapur distance](/distance/shirdi-to-shani-shingnapur/); this page focuses on **how to actually make the trip**.

## Option 1: Private taxi (most popular)

Private taxis are available throughout Shirdi — from taxi stands, hotels, travel desks and app-based services where they operate.

**How it usually works:** you hire a car with a driver for a round trip. Many operators offer a standard "Shani Shingnapur darshan" trip that waits at the temple while you visit and then returns you to Shirdi.

**Before you agree, confirm:**

- The vehicle type and number of passengers
- Whether the price is for a **round trip** including waiting time
- How long the driver will wait at Shingnapur
- Any extra stops you want (for example Rahuri or a meal stop)
- Who pays tolls and parking
- The total price, in writing (a text message is fine)

We do not quote fares because they vary with season, vehicle and demand.

## Option 2: Shared cab or jeep

On busy days, shared vehicles run between Shirdi and Shingnapur, filling up with pilgrims before departing. They are cheaper than a private taxi but less comfortable and less predictable — you may have to wait for the vehicle to fill, and return timings are fixed by the operator. Confirm the return arrangement before paying.

## Option 3: MSRTC bus

State buses connect Shirdi with the region. Depending on the service, you may travel direct or change at Rahuri or Ahilyanagar. Buses are the cheapest option but the slowest. Check timings at the Shirdi bus stand or on the [MSRTC website](https://msrtc.maharashtra.gov.in/), and note the last bus back.

## Option 4: Self-drive

Driving yourself is straightforward: head south through Rahata towards Rahuri, then east towards Sonai and Shingnapur. Expect heavy pilgrim traffic on Saturdays and during festivals. See [by road](/travel/by-road/) and [parking](/travel/parking/).

## Comparing the options

| Option | Comfort | Flexibility | Cost | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Private taxi | High | High | Highest | Families, elders, tight schedules |
| Shared cab / jeep | Medium | Low | Medium | Solo travellers, couples |
| MSRTC bus | Basic | Low | Lowest | Budget travellers with time |
| Self-drive | High | High | Varies | Confident drivers |

## Planning the day

- **Leave early** — ideally around sunrise — to reach Shingnapur before the crowds and the heat.
- **Allow 4–6 hours** for a round trip on a weekday, including darshan. On Saturdays, allow more.
- **Plan Shirdi darshan around Shingnapur**, not the other way round: Shirdi's temple schedule and queues are more fixed. The [Shri Saibaba Sansthan Trust](https://sai.org.in/) publishes its own timings.
- **Eat before or after** — options near the temple are simple. See [food](/travel/food/).

Suggested schedules are in the [one-day itinerary](/pilgrimage/shirdi-shani-shingnapur-one-day-trip/) and the [two-day itinerary](/pilgrimage/shirdi-shani-shingnapur-two-day-itinerary/).

## Avoiding common problems

- **Unofficial "guides" and agents** may approach you in Shirdi or at the temple offering special access. There is no need to pay anyone for darshan beyond official charges.
- **Commission-based shopping stops** are sometimes added by drivers. You can politely decline.
- **Return times**: agree a clear return time with your driver or shared-cab operator, and save their phone number.
