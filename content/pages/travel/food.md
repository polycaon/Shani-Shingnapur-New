## What to expect

Shingnapur is a village, and its food scene reflects that. Near the temple approach you will typically find:

- **Small restaurants** serving vegetarian Maharashtrian meals and thalis.
- **Snack stalls** with tea, *vada pav*, *poha*, *misal* and similar favourites.
- **Sweet shops** selling items that devotees often take home as prasad.

Food near most Hindu pilgrimage sites is vegetarian, and many devotees avoid onion and garlic on fasting days. Choose accordingly if you are travelling with people who observe these customs.

## Prasad and meals from the temple trust

The temple trust is reported to provide prasad and meal facilities (a *prasadalaya*) for devotees. We have **not verified** current timings, arrangements or any charges — ask at the temple on the day or check the [trust's website]({{temple.officialWebsite}}).

## Maharashtrian dishes to try in the region

| Dish | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| Misal pav | Spicy sprouted-bean curry with bread rolls and crunchy toppings |
| Poha | Flattened rice with onion, turmeric and peanuts — a classic breakfast |
| Vada pav | Spiced potato fritter in a bread roll |
| Pithla bhakri | Gram-flour curry with millet flatbread — a rural staple |
| Sabudana khichdi | Tapioca pearls with peanuts — a popular fasting food |
| Puran poli | Sweet flatbread stuffed with lentils and jaggery |

## Eating safely

- **Drink sealed bottled water** or water you trust, especially in summer.
- **Choose busy eateries** where food is freshly made.
- **Carry snacks and water** for queues, particularly with children or elders.
- **Avoid heavy meals before darshan** on hot days if you will be standing for a long time.

## More choice nearby

For a wider range of restaurants, eat in **Shirdi** or **Ahilyanagar** before or after your visit. See [Shirdi](/nearby-places/shirdi/) and [Ahilyanagar](/nearby-places/ahmednagar/).
