## Timing your visit

- **Weekday mornings are calmest.** Saturdays are the busiest day of the week.
- **Check the [festival calendar](/festivals/festival-calendar/)** — Shani Amavasya and Shani Jayanti bring exceptionally large crowds.
- **October to February** is the most comfortable season. **March to May** is very hot, and **June to September** brings monsoon rain.
- **Allow buffer time** for traffic, queues and parking.

More detail: [best time to visit](/blog/best-time-to-visit-shani-shingnapur/).

## Weather

The Deccan plateau around Shingnapur has a hot, dry climate. Summer afternoons are very hot, and the shrine is open-air with little shade. Check the forecast from the [India Meteorological Department](https://mausam.imd.gov.in/) before travelling, especially in summer and the monsoon.

## What to wear

- Modest, comfortable clothing — covering shoulders and knees is a safe choice.
- Slip-on footwear, as you will remove it before entering.
- A cap, scarf or umbrella for sun protection.

## What to carry

- Water, especially in summer
- Small change and UPI on your phone
- Any personal medicines
- A charged phone and power bank
- Photo ID for accommodation

## Money

- UPI is widely used, but keep some cash for small shops, footwear stands and parking.
- Use official trust counters for donations and poojas, and keep receipts.
- There are no verified darshan fees — see [temple timings](/temple/timings/) and the trust's website for current information.

## Safety and scams

- **Touts:** Politely decline anyone offering faster "VIP" access, special pooja packages or guided entry for money outside official channels.
- **Pickpockets:** Keep valuables to a minimum in crowds.
- **Taxis:** Agree the route, waiting time and total price before you set off.
- **Fake websites:** Book trust services only through the trust's own website or office.
- **Astrology pressure:** Be cautious of anyone using fear of Shani Dev to sell expensive rituals or gemstones.

## Visiting with family

- Go early on a weekday; avoid long queues in the heat.
- Keep children close and agree on a meeting point in case you get separated.
- See [visiting with elders and children](/blog/visiting-shani-shingnapur-with-elderly-and-children/).

## Respecting the village

Shani Shingnapur is a living community. Be courteous, avoid photographing people's homes without permission, and take your litter with you.

## Connectivity

Mobile coverage is generally available, but can be patchy in crowds. Download offline maps and save important numbers before you travel.
