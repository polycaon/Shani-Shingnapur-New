## Two layers of practice

It helps to separate two things that are often mixed together:

- **What happens at Shingnapur specifically** — practices shaped by this shrine's form and managed by the temple trust.
- **General Shani worship traditions** — practices followed by devotees of Shani Dev across India, at home and at many temples.

Both are described below, clearly labelled.

## Worship at Shingnapur

### Oil offering (tailabhishek)

The most distinctive act of worship here is offering **oil** to the stone of Shani Dev. The sight of the dark stone glistening with oil is closely associated with the shrine. Oil is sold by shops along the temple approach.

How the oil reaches the stone depends on the trust's arrangements, which can vary with crowd levels: at times devotees have poured oil themselves at the platform; at other times offerings are collected at a designated point or handed to staff. Follow the instructions given on the day.

### Darshan and prayer

For most visitors, worship is simply darshan — a moment of prayer at the platform. See the [darshan guide](/temple/darshan/).

### Special poojas and abhishek

The trust arranges special poojas and abhishek for devotees who request them. We have **not verified** the current list of poojas, their timings or their charges. Please enquire through the trust's website at [shanidev.com]({{temple.officialWebsite}}) or at the trust office in the temple complex, and pay only against an official receipt.

### Aarti

Aarti is performed at set times each day. See [Shani Shingnapur aarti](/temple/aarti/).

## Saturday worship

Saturday is the weekday associated with Shani Dev, and it is the principal day of worship at Shingnapur. The day's significance, and practices such as fasting, are explained in detail in [Saturday worship of Shani Dev](/shani-dev/saturday-worship/). For crowd and travel advice, see [Saturday at Shani Shingnapur](/festivals/saturday/).

## General Shani worship traditions

:::belief In Hindu religious tradition
The following practices are widespread among devotees of Shani Dev. They are matters of faith and custom, and vary from region to region and family to family.
:::

- **Sesame (til) oil.** Offering oil — traditionally sesame oil in western and southern India, mustard oil in parts of the north — is the best-known form of Shani worship. Devotional stories link the practice to Shani Dev being soothed with oil.
- **Dark colours.** Shani Dev is associated with black and deep blue. Devotees may offer black sesame seeds, black gram (urad), or dark cloth, or wear dark clothes on Saturdays.
- **Lighting a lamp.** A lamp lit with sesame oil, often on Saturday evening, is a common household practice.
- **Mantras and stotras.** Devotees recite mantras such as *Om Sham Shanaishcharaya Namah*, or hymns such as the stotra attributed to King Dasharatha. See [Shani mantra](/shani-dev/mantra/) and [Shani stotra](/shani-dev/stotra/).
- **Charity.** Giving to people in need — food, clothes, footwear or oil — on Saturdays is widely regarded as pleasing to Shani Dev, who is associated with justice and with those who labour.
- **Worship of Hanuman.** Many devotees also pray to Hanuman on Saturdays, following a popular belief that Hanuman's devotees are protected from Shani's harsh influence.

## Pooja considerations

- **Intention matters more than expense.** In devotional teaching, a sincere prayer is complete in itself. You do not need to buy elaborate offerings.
- **Be cautious about promises.** Be wary of anyone who claims a paid ritual will *guarantee* relief from Sade Sati, Shani dosha or other problems. Religious practice is a matter of faith; no outcome can be promised. See [understanding Sade Sati](/blog/understanding-sade-sati/).
- **Pay only official charges.** Use trust counters and keep receipts.
- **Plan around crowds.** Arranging a special pooja on Shani Amavasya or Shani Jayanti may be difficult or impossible; ask the trust well in advance.
