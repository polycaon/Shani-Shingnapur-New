## What aarti is

*Aarti* (also spelt *arati*) is one of the most familiar rituals in Hindu worship. A priest waves a lamp — usually with ghee or oil wicks, or burning camphor — in circles before the deity, while devotees sing a hymn, ring bells and clap. At the end, the flame is often brought to the devotees, who pass their hands over it and touch their eyes and forehead, symbolically receiving the deity's light and blessing.

The word comes from the Sanskrit *ārātrika*, associated with the lamp waved before a deity. Aarti usually marks key points in a temple's day — waking the deity at dawn, midday worship and evening worship at dusk.

## When aarti is performed at Shingnapur

Aarti is performed at the shrine at fixed points in the day. Based on reports we have **not yet verified** with the trust:

- **Morning aarti** — commonly reported around 4:30 AM.
- **Midday aarti** — mentioned by some sources around noon.
- **Evening aarti** — commonly reported at sunset, so the clock time changes through the year.

See [temple timings](/temple/timings/) for the full picture and how to confirm current times.

## The aarti song

Aarti is sung to a devotional hymn. In Maharashtra, aartis are usually sung in **Marathi**, and there are well-known Marathi aartis addressed to Shani Dev, alongside Hindi aartis popular across northern India.

We have chosen **not to reproduce aarti lyrics** on this page. Printed and recorded versions differ, authorship is often unclear, and some published versions are under copyright. If you would like to sing along, the simplest and most reliable approach is to use the aarti booklet sold or distributed at the temple, or to follow the priests and devotees on the day.

## What the aarti expresses

Although wording varies, Shani aartis generally:

- **Praise Shani Dev** — often referring to his dark complexion, his parentage as the son of Surya (the Sun) and Chhaya, and his vehicle, the crow.
- **Acknowledge his role as a just judge**, who gives each person the results of their own actions.
- **Ask for mercy and protection**, especially from hardship.
- **Express surrender** — the devotee places their troubles before the deity.

This reflects a wider devotional view of Shani Dev, explained in [about Shani Dev](/shani-dev/about/).

## Taking part respectfully

- **Arrive early.** Aarti draws a crowd, especially on Saturdays. The morning aarti is before dawn.
- **Stand where staff direct you**, and keep aisles clear.
- **Join in as you are comfortable** — singing, clapping or simply watching quietly are all fine.
- **Receive the flame if it is offered**, by passing your palms over it and touching them to your eyes.
- **Avoid flash photography** during the ritual, and follow any rules on photography.

## Aarti at home

Many devotees perform a simple aarti to Shani Dev at home, often on Saturday evenings, with a sesame-oil lamp. Family traditions vary widely; there is no single "correct" form. See [Saturday worship](/shani-dev/saturday-worship/) for more about household practice.
