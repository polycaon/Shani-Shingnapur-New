### Why sources disagree

If you search online you will find several different schedules for Shani Shingnapur. Some travel sites list fixed hours, such as early morning until late evening; others describe the platform as accessible at almost any time. The differences probably come from a mix of real changes over the years, special arrangements on festival days, and websites copying each other. That is why we show the range of reports rather than picking one.

## Darshan

Darshan at Shingnapur is typically a short, direct experience: you join the queue, approach the platform, pray, make an offering if you wish, and move on. On an ordinary weekday morning the whole process can take well under an hour. On Saturdays and festival days, it can take several hours. See the [darshan guide](/temple/darshan/) for step-by-step advice.

## Pooja and abhishek

Devotees traditionally offer oil to Shani Dev, and special poojas can be arranged. The way offerings are handled — whether you pour oil yourself, hand it to staff, or use a designated counter — is decided by the trust and can change with crowd levels. We have **not verified** current pooja schedules or charges. See the [pooja and worship guide](/temple/pooja/).

## Aarti

Aarti is performed at the shrine several times a day. The morning aarti is commonly reported around 4:30 AM and the evening aarti at sunset, which means its clock time shifts through the year. Some sources also mention a midday aarti. See [Shani Shingnapur aarti](/temple/aarti/) for what aarti involves.

## Saturday information

Saturday (*Shanivar*) is dedicated to Shani Dev, and it is by far the busiest regular day:

- Queues build from early morning and stay long through the day.
- Traffic and parking near the village can be slow, especially around midday.
- Long weekends and school holidays add to the crowd.

If your aim is a calm darshan, visit on a weekday. If Saturday worship is important to you, arrive very early and allow extra time. More in [Saturday at Shani Shingnapur](/festivals/saturday/).

## Festival-day considerations

On **[Shani Jayanti](/festivals/shani-jayanti/)** and **[Shani Amavasya](/festivals/shani-amavasya/)** (a new moon falling on a Saturday), crowds can be very large. Special arrangements are often put in place — separate queues, restricted vehicle entry and changed timings. Check the [festival calendar](/festivals/festival-calendar/) for calculated dates, and look for announcements from the trust before you go.

## Crowd considerations by time

| When | What to expect |
| --- | --- |
| Weekday early morning | Usually the calmest time; cooler weather |
| Weekday midday | Moderate crowds; hot from March to May |
| Weekday evening | Busier around the sunset aarti |
| Saturday (any time) | Heavy crowds and long queues |
| Shani Amavasya / Shani Jayanti | Very heavy; special arrangements likely |
| Long weekends & holidays | Heavier than usual, including Sundays |

These are general patterns reported by visitors, not measurements. Conditions on any given day can differ.

## How to confirm timings

1. Check the trust's website: [shanidev.com]({{temple.officialWebsite}}).
2. If you are staying in Shirdi or Ahilyanagar, ask your hotel or taxi operator, who usually hear about changes quickly.
3. On festival days, look for announcements in local Marathi news.

If you learn of a confirmed change, please [tell us](/contact/) so we can update this page.
