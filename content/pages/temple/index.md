## Where the temple is

Shani Shingnapur (also written Shani Shinganapur or Shanishingnapur) is a village in {{temple.taluka}} taluka of {{temple.district}} district, {{temple.state}}. The district was called {{temple.districtFormerName}} until it was officially renamed in {{temple.districtRenamed}}, so you will still see both names on road signs, maps and railway timetables.

The village sits on the dry, open Deccan plateau, {{distance.ahilyanagar}} from Ahilyanagar city and {{distance.shirdi}} from Shirdi by road. The nearest small town is Sonai. For every route in detail, see [how to reach Shani Shingnapur](/travel/how-to-reach/).

## The deity: Shani Dev

The temple is dedicated to **Shani Dev** (also called Shanaishchara or Shaneshwar), the deity associated with the planet Saturn and one of the nine *navagraha* (planetary deities) of Hindu tradition. In devotional belief Shani is the dispenser of the results of one's actions — stern but just — and many people pray to him during difficult periods of life. Read more in our [Shani Dev section](/shani-dev/).

At Shingnapur, Shani Dev is not represented by a carved image. He is worshipped as a **tall, dark stone**, about five and a half to six feet high, standing on a raised, tiled platform. Devotees regard the stone as *swayambhu* — self-manifested rather than made by human hands.

## What makes the shrine unusual

Most Hindu temples house their main deity in an enclosed inner sanctum (*garbhagriha*) under a tower. Shingnapur is different:

- **No roof and no walls.** The stone stands in the open, exposed to sun, wind and rain. According to local tradition, Shani Dev told a villager in a dream that he wished to remain under the open sky, and attempts to build a canopy over him have never succeeded.
- **Worship on the platform.** Devotees traditionally pour oil over the stone as an offering. How close visitors can go, and how offerings are made, depends on the trust's crowd arrangements on the day — see the [darshan guide](/temple/darshan/).
- **A whole village under the deity's protection.** Villagers believe Shani Dev guards Shingnapur, which is why houses traditionally have door frames without locking doors. See [the village without doors](/blog/shani-shingnapur-village-without-doors/).

## Who manages the temple

The shrine is administered by the **{{temple.trust}}**, which runs the temple complex, visitor facilities and, according to its website, services such as accommodation and prasad. ## What visitors should know

- **Saturdays are the busiest day.** Saturday is Shani Dev's day. Queues are longest on Saturday mornings, on [Shani Amavasya](/festivals/shani-amavasya/) and on [Shani Jayanti](/festivals/shani-jayanti/).
- **Timings are not yet verified by us.** Many sources describe the shrine as open for most of the day. See [temple timings](/temple/timings/) for what is reported and how to confirm it.
- **Everyone may now approach the platform.** Since April 2016 the trust has allowed all devotees, including women, onto the platform — subject to crowd control on the day.
- **Dress respectfully.** We have not found a formal published dress code, but modest, clean clothing is customary.
- **Offerings are sold nearby.** Shops along the approach sell oil, flowers and other offerings. Buy only what you need, from shops that display prices.
- **Watch for touts.** No one needs to pay an unofficial "agent" for darshan. Ask temple staff or the trust office if you are unsure about any charge.
