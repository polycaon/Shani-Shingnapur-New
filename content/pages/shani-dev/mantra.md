## What a mantra is

A *mantra* is a sacred sound, word or verse, usually in Sanskrit, repeated as an act of devotion and concentration. Devotees believe that reciting a deity's mantra with attention and faith draws the mind towards that deity. Mantras for Shani Dev are among the most commonly recited in India, particularly on Saturdays.

The mantras below are traditional and have been in use for generations. Pronunciation and small details vary between regions and teachers; if you follow a family or guru tradition, that takes precedence.

## 1. The name mantra

<p class="devanagari" lang="sa">ॐ शं शनैश्चराय नमः</p>

**Transliteration:** Om Sham Shanaishcharaya Namah

**Meaning:** "Om — salutations to Shanaishchara." *Sham* is a seed syllable (*bija*) associated with Shani; *Shanaishchara* means "the slow-moving one", a name of Shani Dev; *namah* means "salutation" or "I bow".

This short mantra is the one most people learn first. It is easy to remember and is often repeated 108 times.

## 2. The beej (seed) mantra

<p class="devanagari" lang="sa">ॐ प्रां प्रीं प्रौं सः शनैश्चराय नमः</p>

**Transliteration:** Om Praam Preem Praum Sah Shanaishcharaya Namah

**Meaning:** The syllables *praam, preem, praum* and *sah* are seed sounds that do not have a dictionary meaning; they are believed to carry the deity's energy. The mantra ends, like the first, with a salutation to Shanaishchara.

This form is often recommended by astrologers during periods associated with Saturn.

## 3. The navagraha verse for Shani

<p class="devanagari" lang="sa">नीलाञ्जनसमाभासं रविपुत्रं यमाग्रजम् ।<br>छायामार्तण्डसम्भूतं तं नमामि शनैश्चरम् ॥</p>

**Transliteration:** Nīlāñjana-samābhāsaṁ ravi-putraṁ yamāgrajam, chhāyā-mārtaṇḍa-sambhūtaṁ taṁ namāmi śanaiścharam.

**Meaning:** "I bow to Shanaishchara, who shines like blue-black collyrium, the son of the Sun and elder brother of Yama, born of Chhaya and Martanda (the Sun)."

This verse is part of the *Navagraha Stotra* traditionally attributed to the sage Vyasa, which has one verse for each of the nine planetary deities. It is often recited alone as a Shani prayer.

## How devotees traditionally recite Shani mantras

:::belief Traditional practice
These are common customs, not rules. Devotees follow them in many different ways.
:::

- **Day:** Saturday is preferred, especially Saturday evening.
- **Count:** 108 repetitions using a *mala* (prayer beads) is common; some people recite 11 or 21 times daily.
- **Setting:** In front of a picture of Shani Dev, a Hanuman shrine, or a lamp lit with sesame oil.
- **Direction:** Some traditions recommend facing west, the direction associated with Shani.
- **Attitude:** Calm, unhurried recitation with attention to meaning is emphasised far more than speed or volume.

## What mantras can — and cannot — promise

Many devotees find that regular recitation brings a sense of calm, discipline and hope, especially in difficult times. That personal experience is real and valuable.

But claims that a mantra will *guarantee* a job, cure an illness, win a court case or remove Sade Sati are not something we can support. Religious practice belongs alongside — not instead of — medical care, sound financial advice and practical effort.
