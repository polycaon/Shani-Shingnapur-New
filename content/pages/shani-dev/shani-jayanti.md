This page explains the religious meaning of Shani Jayanti. If you are planning to be at Shingnapur on the day, see [Shani Jayanti at Shani Shingnapur](/festivals/shani-jayanti/) for crowd and travel advice.

## What Shani Jayanti celebrates

*Jayanti* means the day of a deity's birth or appearance. **Shani Jayanti** honours the appearance of Shani Dev, son of Surya and Chhaya. For devotees it is the most important day of the year for Shani worship — a day for prayer, charity, and seeking the deity's grace.

## How the date is set

Shani Jayanti follows the **lunar calendar**, so its date on the Gregorian calendar changes every year.

- It falls on **Amavasya**, the new-moon day.
- In the **amanta** calendar used in Maharashtra, Gujarat and much of south India — where months end on the new moon — it is **Vaishakha Amavasya**.
- In the **purnimanta** calendar used in much of north India — where months end on the full moon — the same day is called **Jyeshtha Amavasya**.

The two names describe the **same day**. It usually falls in late May or early June.

## How Shani Jayanti is observed

:::belief Traditional observances
Practices vary by region, community and family. The following are widely followed.
:::

- **Temple visits.** Devotees throng Shani shrines — including Shingnapur, which sees one of its largest crowds of the year.
- **Oil abhishek.** Offering sesame or mustard oil to Shani Dev's image or stone.
- **Fasting.** Some devotees fast for the day, or eat a single simple meal.
- **Mantras and stotras.** Reciting Shani mantras, the Dasharatha Shani Stotra, the Shani Chalisa or the 108 names. See [Shani mantra](/shani-dev/mantra/) and [stotra](/shani-dev/stotra/).
- **Havan (fire ritual).** Some temples and families perform a *Shani shanti* havan.
- **Charity.** Giving food, clothes, footwear, black sesame, oil or blankets to people in need is considered especially meritorious.
- **Worship of Hanuman.** Following the traditional story of Hanuman and Shani, many devotees also visit Hanuman temples.

## When Shani Jayanti falls on a Saturday

When Shani Jayanti falls on a Saturday, it is also a [Shani Amavasya](/festivals/shani-amavasya/) — a combination devotees regard as especially significant, and one that usually brings exceptionally large crowds.

## Coinciding observances

In the purnimanta calendar, Jyeshtha Amavasya is also observed by many married women in north India as **Vat Savitri Vrat**. In Maharashtra, Vat Purnima is observed on the following full moon instead. You may see both festivals mentioned together in north Indian calendars.

## A note on meaning

Beyond ritual, many teachers describe Shani Jayanti as a day to reflect on one's own conduct — Shani being the deity of karma — and to renew commitments to honesty, discipline and compassion.
