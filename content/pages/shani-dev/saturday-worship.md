For advice on visiting Shingnapur on a Saturday — crowds, queues and timing — see [Saturday at Shani Shingnapur](/festivals/saturday/). This page explains the devotional practice.

## Why Saturday?

The seven-day week in Indian tradition names each day after a celestial body. **Shanivar** — Saturday — is named after Shani, just as the English "Saturday" is named after Saturn. It is therefore the natural day for Shani worship, and has been for a very long time.

## How devotees observe Saturday

:::belief Traditional practice
Customs differ between families and regions. None of these practices is compulsory, and devotees choose what suits them.
:::

### Morning

- A bath before prayers, sometimes with a few black sesame seeds in the water.
- Wearing dark clothes — black or deep blue — in some households.
- Reciting a Shani mantra such as *Om Sham Shanaishcharaya Namah*. See [Shani mantra](/shani-dev/mantra/).

### Temple visit

- Visiting a Shani temple — or, very commonly, a **Hanuman temple**, in keeping with the story that Hanuman's devotees are protected from Shani's harshness.
- Offering sesame or mustard oil, black sesame, black gram (urad), flowers or dark cloth.

### The Saturday fast (Shanivar vrat)

Some devotees fast on Saturdays, sometimes for a set number of weeks (such as 7, 11 or 19) as a personal vow. Forms vary — from a complete fast to a single meal after sunset, often avoiding salt or taking simple food made with sesame or urad.

:::warning Health first
Fasting is not suitable for everyone. People who are pregnant, elderly, diabetic, on medication or otherwise unwell should seek medical advice before fasting. No religious tradition requires anyone to endanger their health.
:::

### Evening

- Lighting a lamp with sesame oil, sometimes under a *peepal* (sacred fig) tree, a practice followed in many parts of India.
- Reciting a stotra such as the Dasharatha Shani Stotra or the Shani Chalisa. See [Shani stotra](/shani-dev/stotra/).

### Charity

Giving on Saturdays — food, oil, clothes, footwear or help to labourers and the elderly — is widely considered one of the most meaningful forms of Shani worship, reflecting his association with justice and with those who work hard for little reward.

## Things devotees traditionally avoid on Saturdays

Some families observe customs such as not buying iron objects, oil or salt on Saturdays, or not cutting hair and nails. These are **folk customs** that vary widely; they are not universal rules, and many devotees do not follow them.

## Saturday at Shingnapur

At [Shani Shingnapur](/temple/), Saturday is the busiest regular day of the week. If you want to worship there on a Saturday, arrive very early and be prepared for long queues. [Plan your visit](/plan-your-visit/) or read the [darshan guide](/temple/darshan/).
