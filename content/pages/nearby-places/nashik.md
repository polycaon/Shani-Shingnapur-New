## Why visit

Nashik is one of Maharashtra's great religious cities. It stands on the **Godavari**, one of India's most sacred rivers, and is traditionally associated with events of the **Ramayana**: the Panchavati area is where Rama, Sita and Lakshmana are said to have lived during their exile. Nashik is also one of the four sites of the **Kumbh Mela**, held here (together with Trimbakeshwar) as the Simhastha Kumbh roughly every twelve years.

## Things to do

- **Ramkund** — the most sacred bathing ghat on the Godavari, where pilgrims take a holy dip and perform rites for ancestors.
- **Panchavati** — the area linked with the Ramayana, including temples such as the **Kalaram Temple** and the **Sita Gufa** cave shrine.
- **The ghats** — evening along the river is atmospheric, with lamps and prayers.
- **Pandavleni Caves** — an ancient group of Buddhist rock-cut caves on a hill on the city's edge.
- **Vineyards** — the Nashik region is known for grape-growing and wineries, for those interested.

## Pilgrimage relevance

Nashik is the natural base for **[Trimbakeshwar](/nearby-places/trimbakeshwar/)**, and lies on a convenient route to Shirdi and Shani Shingnapur. It fits well at the start of a west-to-east [Maharashtra temple tour](/pilgrimage/maharashtra-temple-tour/).

## Suggested duration

A full day for Nashik, plus half to a full day for Trimbakeshwar.

## Things to know

- **Kumbh Mela years** bring enormous crowds to Nashik and Trimbakeshwar. The next Simhastha Kumbh is expected in 2027; check official announcements for dates and arrangements.
- **Monsoon** swells the Godavari; ghat access can be restricted when water levels are high.

## How it connects with Shani Shingnapur

Drive east via Sinnar to Shirdi, then south via Rahuri to Shingnapur. See [from Nashik](/travel/from-nashik/).
