## Why visit

Most visitors pass through Ahilyanagar on the way to Shingnapur — but the city rewards a short stop. It was founded at the end of the fifteenth century by **Ahmad Nizam Shah**, founder of the Nizam Shahi dynasty, and served as its capital. In October 2024 the city and district were officially renamed **Ahilyanagar** in honour of **Ahilyabai Holkar**, the eighteenth-century ruler of Indore, who was born in the district.

## Things to see

- **Ahmednagar Fort** — a large, historic fort associated with the Nizam Shahi dynasty. During the Quit India Movement (1942–45), Jawaharlal Nehru and other national leaders were imprisoned here, and Nehru wrote *The Discovery of India* during his detention. Parts of the fort are within a military area, so access can be restricted; check before you go.
- **Cavalry Tank Museum** — a museum of armoured vehicles associated with the Indian Army's Armoured Corps Centre and School.
- **Meherabad** — a short distance south of the city, the site of the samadhi (tomb-shrine) of the spiritual teacher Meher Baba, visited by followers from around the world.
- **Salabat Khan's tomb** (often called Chand Bibi's Palace) — a hilltop tomb with wide views, on the city's outskirts.

Opening hours and access rules at these sites change; please check locally.

## Pilgrimage relevance

For pilgrims, Ahilyanagar is mainly a **practical base**: the best-connected railway station near Shingnapur, a major MSRTC bus hub, and a good range of hotels and restaurants.

## Suggested duration

Two to four hours for the main sights, or an overnight stay if you are arriving by train.

## How it connects with Shani Shingnapur

Shingnapur is north-east of the city, around an hour's drive. See [Ahilyanagar to Shani Shingnapur](/distance/ahmednagar-to-shani-shingnapur/).
