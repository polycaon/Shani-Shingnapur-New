## Why visit

The **Trimbakeshwar Temple** enshrines one of the twelve **Jyotirlingas**, the most sacred shrines of Shiva. Its linga is distinctive: tradition describes it as having three faces representing Brahma, Vishnu and Shiva. The present stone temple, built in the eighteenth century, is commonly attributed to the Peshwa Balaji Bajirao (Nanasaheb).

The town sits at the foot of the **Brahmagiri** hills, where the **Godavari** is traditionally said to originate. The **Kushavarta** tank in the town is regarded as the river's symbolic source and is an important bathing place, especially during the Kumbh Mela.

Trimbakeshwar is also known for certain rites that families travel here to perform, such as rituals for ancestors. Arrange these only through recognised priests and agree any charges in advance.

## Things to do

- Darshan at the Jyotirlinga temple.
- Visit Kushavarta tank.
- For the fit and energetic, the trek up **Brahmagiri** to shrines associated with the Godavari's source (avoid in heavy monsoon rain).
- Explore the small temple town and its ghats.

## Suggested duration

Half a day for darshan; a full day with Brahmagiri.

## Things to know

- **Queues** can be very long on Mondays, in the month of Shravan (usually July–August) and on Mahashivratri.
- **Temple rules** on dress and entry to inner areas are set by the temple management; check current guidance before you go.
- **Kumbh Mela** years bring very large crowds.
