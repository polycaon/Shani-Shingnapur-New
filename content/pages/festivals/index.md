## The ritual year at Shingnapur

Shani Dev's calendar is simple. There is one great annual festival — **Shani Jayanti** — and a recurring pattern of important days tied to the **week** (Saturday) and the **moon** (Amavasya). When the two coincide, on a Saturday new moon, the result is **Shani Amavasya**, one of the most crowded days of all.

## Dates change every year

Shani Jayanti and Shani Amavasya follow the Hindu lunar calendar, so their dates on the Gregorian calendar change each year. Rather than printing dates that will go out of date, our [festival calendar](/festivals/festival-calendar/) calculates them for the current and coming years, clearly labelled as calculated.

## Planning around festivals

| If you want… | Then… |
| --- | --- |
| To worship on a special day | Arrive very early, expect long queues, and book accommodation well ahead |
| A calm, unhurried visit | Choose a weekday that is not an Amavasya |
| To avoid traffic | Avoid the day before and after major festivals, as well as the day itself |

On festival days, the trust and police often introduce special arrangements — separate queues, vehicle restrictions and changed timings. Check for announcements before you travel.
