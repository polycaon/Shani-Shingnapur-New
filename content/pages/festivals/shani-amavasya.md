## What is Shani Amavasya?

*Amavasya* is the new-moon day, the last day of the dark fortnight in the Hindu lunar calendar. It is traditionally associated with remembrance of ancestors, charity and worship. **Shani Amavasya** — also called *Shanishchari Amavasya* — is an Amavasya that falls on a **Saturday**, Shani Dev's own day.

## Why it matters to devotees

:::belief In Hindu religious tradition
The combination of Shani's weekday with the new moon is regarded by devotees as an especially powerful time to worship Shani Dev. People who associate a difficult period in their lives with Shani — such as Sade Sati — often make a special effort to worship on this day. These are matters of faith; no particular outcome can be promised.
:::

Common observances include oil abhishek, reciting Shani mantras and stotras, charity, and rites for ancestors that are traditionally performed on any Amavasya.

## How often does it occur?

There are about twelve or thirteen new moons a year, and roughly one in seven falls on a Saturday. In practice that means Shani Amavasya usually occurs **one to three times a year** — and in some years not at all.

See the [festival calendar](/festivals/festival-calendar/) for calculated dates.

## What visitors can expect at Shingnapur

- **Very heavy crowds**, often comparable to Shani Jayanti.
- **Queues from very early morning** and throughout the day.
- **Traffic control and parking restrictions** around the village.
- **Special arrangements** by the temple trust, which may include changes to timings and queue routes.

## Travel planning

- **Book accommodation early** — especially if Shani Amavasya falls on a long weekend.
- **Arrive before dawn** if you want to worship on the day.
- **Allow extra travel time** from Shirdi, Ahilyanagar and beyond.
- **Consider a nearby weekday** if you prefer a calm visit.
