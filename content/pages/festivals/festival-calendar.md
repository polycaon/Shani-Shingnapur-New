## How these dates are calculated

Hindu festivals such as Shani Jayanti follow the **lunar calendar**. Instead of printing dates that go out of date, this calendar calculates them from the positions of the Sun and Moon:

- **Amavasya** is the lunar day (*tithi*) that ends at the astronomical new moon. We treat as the observance day the first civil day, in Indian Standard Time, whose sunrise falls within the Amavasya tithi — a common panchang convention. When the tithi falls entirely between two sunrises, we use the day containing most of it.
- **Shani Amavasya** is any Amavasya whose observance day is a Saturday.
- **Shani Jayanti** is the Amavasya that ends the lunar month of Vaishakha in the amanta calendar: the first new moon after the Sun enters the sidereal sign of Vrishabha (Taurus).

:::verify Always confirm before you travel
Panchangs differ in their exact conventions (for example, the reference location and sunrise rule). Occasionally they place an Amavasya one day earlier or later than our calculation. Treat these dates as a planning guide and confirm with a local panchang or the temple trust. When a date is confirmed from an authoritative source, we mark it as such.
:::

## Every Saturday is special

Remember that **every Saturday** is Shani Dev's day and brings large crowds. See [Saturday at Shani Shingnapur](/festivals/saturday/).

## Other busy periods

- **Long weekends and school holidays** increase crowds at Shingnapur and Shirdi.
- **Major Shirdi festivals** — such as Ram Navami, Guru Purnima and Vijayadashami — increase traffic across the region.
