For the religious meaning of the day and how it is observed across India, see [Shani Jayanti: meaning and observance](/shani-dev/shani-jayanti/). This page is about **being at Shingnapur** on the day.

## When is Shani Jayanti?

Shani Jayanti falls on the **Amavasya (new moon) that ends the lunar month of Vaishakha** in the amanta calendar used in Maharashtra — the same day north Indian calendars call Jyeshtha Amavasya. That is usually in **late May or early June**.

Our [festival calendar](/festivals/festival-calendar/) calculates the date for the current and coming years. Because panchangs can differ by a day depending on local sunrise and convention, confirm the date with a local panchang or the temple trust.

## What happens at the temple

:::info What visitors typically report
Specific programmes are decided by the temple trust each year, and we have not verified the current schedule. Visitors typically describe the following.
:::

- **Crowds from before dawn**, with devotees arriving from across Maharashtra and beyond.
- **Special worship and abhishek** of Shani Dev, along with aartis through the day.
- **Religious programmes** such as recitations, bhajans and, in some years, a *yajna* or havan.
- **Distribution of prasad** to devotees.
- **A festive atmosphere** in the village, with many stalls and vendors.

## Crowds and arrangements

Shani Jayanti is one of the busiest days of the year. You can expect:

- **Long queues** — possibly several hours.
- **Vehicle restrictions** near the village, with parking further out and a walk in.
- **Barricaded queue routes**, managed by volunteers and police.
- **Heat** — late May and early June are among the hottest weeks of the year on the Deccan plateau.

## Planning your visit

1. **Confirm the date** — see the [festival calendar](/festivals/festival-calendar/).
2. **Book accommodation early** — rooms in the village and in Shirdi fill up. See [accommodation](/travel/accommodation/).
3. **Arrive before dawn**, or consider visiting on a quieter day just before or after.
4. **Carry water, sun protection and snacks.** Wear light, modest clothing.
5. **Keep elders and children comfortable.** If queues are too long, a short prayer from a distance is a perfectly valid darshan.
6. **Travel with a local driver** who knows the diversions. See [parking](/travel/parking/).
7. **Stay alert to touts** offering paid shortcuts.

## If Shani Jayanti falls on a Saturday

When Shani Jayanti falls on a Saturday, it is also a [Shani Amavasya](/festivals/shani-amavasya/). Expect even larger crowds.

## A quieter alternative

Many devotees choose to visit on an ordinary weekday close to Shani Jayanti, and observe the day itself at home or at a local temple with mantras, stotras and charity. See [Saturday worship](/shani-dev/saturday-worship/) for household practices.
