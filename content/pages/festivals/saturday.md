## Why Saturdays are special

Saturday — *Shanivar* — is named after Shani, and devotees throughout India worship Shani Dev on this day. At Shingnapur, one of his most famous shrines, that means a weekly surge of pilgrims. For the devotional background, see [Saturday worship of Shani Dev](/shani-dev/saturday-worship/).

## What to expect

- **Crowds from early morning.** Queues build from before sunrise and stay long through the day.
- **Traffic.** Approach roads from Shirdi, Ahilyanagar and Sonai can be slow, especially from mid-morning.
- **Parking further out.** You may need to walk further. See [parking](/travel/parking/).
- **Busier shops and eateries.** More choice, but also more crowding.
- **Longer waits on long weekends** and during school holidays.

If a Saturday coincides with an Amavasya, it becomes a [Shani Amavasya](/festivals/shani-amavasya/) — among the busiest days of the year.

## Tips for a Saturday visit

1. **Arrive at or before dawn.** The earliest hours are the most manageable.
2. **Stay nearby the night before** — in the village or in Shirdi. See [accommodation](/travel/accommodation/).
3. **Travel light** and keep valuables to a minimum.
4. **Carry water and sun protection.**
5. **Agree a meeting point** with your group in case you are separated.
6. **Follow queue instructions**, and be patient with staff and fellow devotees.

## Saturday or weekday?

| | Saturday | Weekday |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Religious significance | Shani Dev's own day | Any day is suitable for darshan |
| Crowds | Heavy | Usually lighter |
| Queue time | Long | Often short |
| Good for | Devotees for whom Saturday worship is important | First-time visitors, families, elders |

Both are valid choices — it depends on what matters most to you.
