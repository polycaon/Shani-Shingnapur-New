## Why pilgrims combine the two

Shirdi, home of the Samadhi Mandir of **Sai Baba of Shirdi**, is one of India's most visited pilgrimage towns. Shani Shingnapur is close enough to visit on the same trip, and for many families the two have become a natural pair: Sai Baba's message of faith and patience (*shraddha* and *saburi*), and Shani Dev's association with justice, discipline and endurance.

There is no religious rule that the two must be visited together, or in a particular order. It is simply a well-established pilgrim tradition — and a practical one, given the short distance.

## Which to visit first?

There is no fixed rule. Practical considerations usually decide:

- **Shingnapur first, then Shirdi** — Leave Shirdi at dawn, reach Shingnapur before the crowds, return for an afternoon or evening in Shirdi. Good for weekdays.
- **Shirdi first, then Shingnapur** — Take early-morning Sai Baba darshan, then drive to Shingnapur. Good if you have a fixed darshan slot or aarti you want to attend in Shirdi.

Shirdi's schedule — aartis, queue systems and any pass arrangements — is set by the Shri Saibaba Sansthan Trust and is usually the less flexible of the two. Plan around it, and check [sai.org.in](https://sai.org.in/) for current arrangements.

## Transport between the two

A private taxi round trip from Shirdi is the most common choice. Shared cabs and MSRTC buses are cheaper but slower. Full details in [from Shirdi](/travel/from-shirdi/).

## Avoiding the biggest crowds

- **Avoid Saturday at Shingnapur** and **Thursday at Shirdi** if you want shorter queues.
- **Long weekends and school holidays** are busy at both.
- **Check the [festival calendar](/festivals/festival-calendar/)** for Shani Amavasya and Shani Jayanti. Major Sai Baba festivals, such as Guru Purnima and Ram Navami, also bring huge crowds to Shirdi.
