A visit to Shani Shingnapur is simple, but it is different from most temple visits. These are the things first-time visitors most often wish they had known.

## 1. There is no temple building around the deity

Expect an **open-air platform**, not a sanctum. Shani Dev is worshipped as a tall, dark stone under the open sky. The simplicity is deliberate, rooted in local tradition — see the [temple history](/temple/history/). Around the platform is a complex with queue areas, counters and other shrines.

## 2. Saturdays are a different experience

Saturday is Shani Dev's day. It is the most significant day for many devotees and by far the busiest. On a weekday you may finish darshan quickly; on a Saturday, allow hours. See [Saturday at Shani Shingnapur](/festivals/saturday/).

## 3. Confirm timings before you go

Online sources give different timings, and we haven't yet verified a current schedule with the trust. Check the trust's website or ask locally. See [timings](/temple/timings/).

## 4. Everyone may approach the platform

Since April 2016, the trust has allowed all devotees — women and men — onto the platform, subject to crowd arrangements on the day.

## 5. You'll remove your footwear — and the ground can be hot

Wear footwear you can slip off easily, and be ready for hot stone in summer. Visiting early in the morning helps.

## 6. Offerings are optional

Shops along the approach sell oil and other offerings for Shani Dev. You can buy them if you wish, but a sincere prayer is complete on its own. Buy from shops that display prices. See the [pooja guide](/temple/pooja/).

## 7. You don't need an agent

You may be approached by people offering faster darshan, special poojas or "VIP" access for money. You do not need them. Use official trust counters, and pay only against receipts.

## 8. Dress modestly

We have not found a formal published dress code, but modest, comfortable clothing is appropriate and respectful.

## 9. The village is a living community

The famous door-free houses are people's homes. Be respectful and ask before photographing. See [the village without doors](/blog/shani-shingnapur-village-without-doors/).

## 10. Plan transport both ways

Shingnapur has no station or airport. If you come by taxi, agree the round trip, waiting time and total price in advance. If you come by bus, know when the last one leaves. See [how to reach](/travel/how-to-reach/).

## 11. Food is simple

Expect simple vegetarian meals and snacks near the temple. See [food](/travel/food/).

## 12. It pairs naturally with Shirdi

Many visitors combine Shingnapur with Shirdi, {{distance.shirdi}} away. See the [Shirdi + Shani Shingnapur guide](/pilgrimage/shirdi-shani-shingnapur/).
