## Shani worship in Maharashtra

Shani Dev is honoured in many ways across Maharashtra. Alongside a few famous shrines are countless small ones — a Shani image in a village temple, a stone under a tree, or a Shani shrine beside a temple to Hanuman, whom Marathi speakers often call **Maruti**.

This Maruti–Shani pairing reflects a popular story: Hanuman once freed Shani, and Shani promised not to trouble Hanuman's devotees. As a result, Saturday worship in Maharashtra very often means visiting a Maruti temple as well as, or instead of, a Shani shrine. See [Saturday worship](/shani-dev/saturday-worship/).

## Shani Shingnapur

The best known of all is **[Shani Shingnapur](/temple/)** in {{temple.taluka}} taluka, {{temple.district}} district. Its open-air platform, where Shani Dev is worshipped as a self-manifested stone, and its door-free village tradition make it unique. It draws pilgrims from across India, especially on Saturdays, Shani Amavasya and Shani Jayanti.

## Other Shani shrines in Maharashtra

Maharashtra has several other Shani shrines with regional followings. Among those frequently mentioned by devotees are shrines at **Rakshasbhuvan** in Beed district and **Nastanpur** in Nashik district.

## Well-known Shani temples elsewhere in India

For readers interested in Shani temples beyond Maharashtra:

| Temple | Location | Why it is known |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Thirunallar Saniswaran Temple (Dharbaranyeswarar Temple) | Karaikal district, Puducherry | One of the Navagraha temples of the Kaveri delta, closely associated with Shani |
| Kokilavan Shani Dham | Near Kosi Kalan, Mathura district, Uttar Pradesh | Associated with Krishna traditions; draws large Saturday crowds |
| Shani Dham | Delhi | A large modern temple complex in south Delhi |

Each has its own traditions and arrangements; check with the temple before planning a visit.
